# Foundations of Machine Learning for Materials Science


For a very long time, finding a new material has meant long hours in the lab: mix something 🧪, heat it🌡️, test it 🔬, and try again. This works, but it is slow. Now, machine learning and AI are starting to change the game. 💻Instead of testing one idea at a time, computers can look at huge amounts of past data and quickly point us toward materials that are likely to work well. Some AI tools can even suggest brand new materials that no one has made before. This does not remove the scientist from the picture - it gives the scientist a much faster and smarter starting point 🧑‍🔬.

Almost everywhere you look, AI is helping researchers design better batteries🔋 and cleaner energy storage, find lighter and stronger metal alloys🚀, speed up the search for new catalysts, and discover materials for solar cells☀️ , medicines💊, and everyday plastics. In each of these areas, AI helps cut down the huge number of possible materials to a short, promising list - saving years of work. AI is also helping with materials characterization, synthesis recipe suggestions, and process management, among other things. Some labs are even going a step further, using "self-driving labs," where robots and AI work together to plan and run experiments with very little human input - a nice example of AI changing not just what we discover, but how we discover it.

This is exactly why it matters so much for you, as a future materials scientist, to feel comfortable with AI. The materials scientists of tomorrow will not need to choose between knowing their chemistry and physics, or knowing how to use data and AI tools - they will need both 🔨🔧. The people who can combine strong materials science with good data skills will be the ones leading the most exciting discoveries in the years ahead. Being "AI-ready" is quickly becoming just as normal a skill as knowing how to use a microscope or run an experiment.

This is where our course begins. We will start from the ground up: understanding what machine learning actually is, how it can be used with materials data, and how to think about problems in a way that AI tools can help with. From there, we will build up, step by step, toward the kinds of real tools and methods used in materials discovery today.

No need to worry if this all sounds new. By the end of this course, you will have a solid, practical foundation - and hopefully a bit of excitement about where this field is heading.
